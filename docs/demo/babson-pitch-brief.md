# Babson demo delivery brief

Owner context: tomorrow's AI for Entrepreneurship Educators workshop mini hackathon, approximately 45 academics. Prioritize a memorable, credible classroom-ready demonstration and dependable delivery. Three minutes is a working rehearsal length; provide a 90-second version and a five-minute expansion. This is a proposed pitch, not a claim about judging criteria or guaranteed competition results.

## The story

**First Ten helps students turn real customer conversations into a next decision they can explain.**

AI makes it easier to build something. A polished prototype still leaves the harder classroom question: what did the student learn from people, and how did it change what they will do? First Ten connects the conversation, the observation, the decision and the next test. AI helps draft and organize; people provide the evidence and make the decision.

Educators get a teachable process, a reusable lesson and a way to assess reasoning. The implementation must show which review/class features are live and which remain planned. No claims of learning gains, customer acquisition or market validation without real studies.

## Fictional stage demonstration: StudyLoop

Clearly label all example people, quotes and results **fictional demo data**. Never count them as participant research or production adoption.

- Starting idea: a campus app to help students find study partners.
- Starting assumption: students struggle to find enough people to study with.
- Maya describes groups falling apart because nobody knows who will actually turn up: “I don't need more people. I need to know who's actually showing up.”
- Owen describes repeated last-minute scheduling messages. Priya describes an existing group that works well when attendance is clear.
- Counterevidence: Nora already has a reliable group and does not want another app. Preserve this disagreement rather than flattening everyone into a target user.
- Observation: coordination and commitment may matter more for a specific group than finding additional partners. This is an interpretation of fictional inputs, not a research result.
- Decision: narrow the proposed audience to existing groups with unreliable attendance; test a simple confirmation flow before building a new discovery feed.
- Next experiment: invite five consenting groups to try confirmations for a week and compare their own attendance records. Mark this proposed; no invented completion, payment or improvement percentage.

The interface should let a presenter inspect a source excerpt, see an alternative explanation, select a decision and preview the next test. Reset must be obvious and deterministic. The core public demo must work without login, network AI or changing database records.

## Stage reliability and quality bar

- A public demo entry and direct shareable URL work without email sign-in.
- One-click reset returns to the same known example; refresh never loses the presenter.
- Readable at projected desktop sizes and on phones; strong visible focus and no color-only meaning.
- Clear fictional/draft/proposed labels, useful empty/error states and no fabricated testimonials.
- No requirement for a live paid model call to complete the pitch.
- Keep a short static narrative and source excerpts available as fallback.
- Real app sign-in and data remain available alongside the public example.
- Do not claim Babson endorsement or use its brand as First Ten's identity.

## What makes the product worth showing

The distinctive demo is the continuity of reasoning, not the volume of generated text: a source can challenge an assumption, a student can explain a choice, and an educator can teach and review that process. Every feature should make that sequence clearer or more dependable.
